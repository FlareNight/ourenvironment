Living Organisms

Biotic Factors

Biotic components refer to all the living beings present in an ecosystem. They are typically categorized into producers (autotrophs), consumers (heterotrophs), and decomposers (saprotrophs).

Producers Plants, algae, cyanobacteria capturing solar energy.
Consumers Herbivores, carnivores, and omnivores feeding on others.

Abiotic Factors

Abiotic components are the non-living chemical and physical parts of the environment that affect living organisms and the functioning of ecosystems.

Climate & Light Temperature, sunlight duration, humidity, and rainfall.

Human Intervention

Environmental Impact & Biomagnification

Understand how pollutants and toxic chemicals magnify in concentration as they move up trophic levels.

science

Biological Magnification

Non-biodegradable chemicals such as pesticides (DDT) and heavy metals increase in concentration at each successive trophic level.

Apex Impact Highest toxin concentration found in top carnivores and birds of prey.
view_in_ar_new

Ozone Layer Depletion

Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) release chlorine atoms in the stratosphere, breaking down protective ozone molecules and increasing UV radiation.

Consequence Skin cancer risks, reduced phytoplankton productivity, and climate shifts.

Waste & Garbage Disposal

Accumulation of non-biodegradable plastics and electronic waste choking terrestrial and marine ecosystems, destroying natural habitats.

Solution Adopting the 4 R's: Refuse, Reduce, Reuse, and Recycle.
